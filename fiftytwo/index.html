The book

The work of a
lifetime.

What if fatherhood isn't something you balance against the rest of your life, but the thing you build your life around? 52 Saturdays puts that idea into practice.

What is it?

52 Saturdays is part philosophy, part field guide and part yearlong challenge. Each week gives you a new perspective on fatherhood and a mission to put it into practice with your kids.

Who is it for?

It's for the dad who loves being a dad but wants more from it. More connection, more adventure, more laughter and more of the life happening right in front of him.

Why does it matter?

You get roughly 900 Saturdays from the day your child is born until they turn eighteen. Those ordinary days add up to an entire childhood. What you do with them matters.

What makes it different?

This isn't a manual for managing your kids. It's a challenge to grow alongside them, drawing on the science of connection, the power of play and the art of living well.

The dad behind it

Hey, I'm Case.

I'm raising boys who know they are deeply loved and wildly capable.

I want them to feel secure in who they are, curious about the world and courageous enough to explore it. And I want to become more of those things myself.

I see fatherhood as my life's work. 52 Saturdays is how I put that into practice.

Inside 52 Saturdays

Four movements. One extraordinary year.Hover or tap a card to open it

  1. Reclaim wonder

    Get good at being curious.

    Movement 01 · Weeks 1–13

    Reclaim wonder

    Rediscover the world through your kids' eyes. Make space for curiosity, unstructured play and the kind of fun that doesn't need a reason.

  2. Build the bond

    Give them somewhere to belong.

    Movement 02 · Weeks 14–26

    Build the bond

    Get to know the person your child is becoming. Build trust through shared experiences, real conversations and the everyday practice of showing up.

  3. Chase the extraordinary

    Find out what you're capable of.

    Movement 03 · Weeks 27–39

    Chase the extraordinary

    Take on something bigger together. Show your kids what courage, persistence and adventure look like when you're learning right alongside them.

  4. Create the legacy

    Make the good stuff stick.

    Movement 04 · Weeks 40–52

    Create the legacy

    Turn a year of shared experiences into something lasting. Create traditions, preserve your stories and decide what your family wants to carry forward.
